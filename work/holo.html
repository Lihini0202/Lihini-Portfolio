Holo: seeing when a chat goes cold

Everyone jokes about being ghosted. I studied it properly, built a model that predicts ghosting risk, and designed an app that turns that research into something people can use.

The problem

Ghosting feels random, but it isn't

Conversations on dating apps often end without warning. My research showed there are patterns in how people interact before that happens. The challenge was showing a risk prediction in a way that feels helpful, not judgmental.

Who it is for

People using dating apps

Anyone stuck wondering whether a match is losing interest. They paste the chat or upload a screenshot, and Holo shows a risk level with the signals behind it, so they can decide what to do next instead of overthinking it.

What I did

From research paper to working product

  • Built a behavioural ML model from user interaction data, trained on 19,061 conversations. The paper is under review at Elsevier.
  • Designed six screens in Figma: Analyze, Search, Coach, Insights, Validate and Profile.
  • Built the app in Flutter, with a FastAPI backend, Celery workers for slow OCR and model calls, and Supabase for data and sign-in.
  • Locked down every table with row-level security so people only ever see their own conversations.

Key decisions

Why it looks the way it does

Risk bands instead of percentages

Even after calibration the model could be off by about 5 points, so a precise-looking "73%" would promise more than it knows. Bands like "Some warning signs" are honest and easier to read.

Show the reasons, not just the verdict

Each result lists the signals behind it, such as shorter replies or fewer questions back, so the score never feels like a black box.

Kind wording, and a coach

The app suggests what to do next instead of blaming anyone. The Coach screen helps rewrite a message that is unlikely to get a reply.

Ask what really happened

About two weeks after an analysis, Holo asks whether the prediction was right. Those answers will let me check the model against real outcomes and retune it.

Under the hood

A simple model, on purpose

The model is a logistic regression using three signals: how long their messages are compared to yours, how often they ask questions back, and how steady their reply times are. I tried gradient boosting, but it ranked a clearly disengaged chat as safer than a mildly disengaged one. The simpler model keeps that order right, and CI checks it on all 20 input combinations.

I also left out message count, even though it scored slightly higher on its own (0.924 AUC). Pasted excerpts are short no matter how the chat is going, so with it a healthy conversation scored 0.73 risk. Without it, the same chat scored 0.01.
